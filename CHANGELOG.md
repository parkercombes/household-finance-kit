# Changelog

All notable changes to `household-finance-kit` will be documented in this file.

The format is based on Keep a Changelog, and this project is expected to use semantic versioning once releases begin.

## [Unreleased]

### Fixed

- CI lint: pin `ruff==0.16.10` in the dev extras. CI installed the newest ruff (0.17.5), whose changed default rules flagged the `noqa` directives 0.16.10 requires, so CI had failed at the lint step since 2026-10-28.
- M3-07 parity run (2026-10-28, July 2026 against the household's Google Sheets download): every bucket total reconciled with the household's scripted statement, each difference a declared judgement call or per-transaction cent reconciliation; the run exposed that Tiller's Sheets feed writes `ACTIVE` in Balance History's Account Status, which the `hfk init` interview filed as closed. Only an explicit closed-style status (`closed`, `inactive`) files an account under `closed_accounts` now; a missing or unknown status counts as open, as ADR-0014 says closure is declared, never inferred. Remaining interview gaps are roadmap row `M5-17`.

### Changed

- M5-18 (ADR-0015): the `HFK Plan` sheet is the only plan source. `plan.yaml` is no longer read: one left in an instance is an error naming the fix, and a `plan:` key in `household.yaml` is an unknown-key error. Removed: the `plan.yaml` loader, `plan_source`, the unused-file warning, `load_plan`, `check_plan`, `unused_plan_file`, and the `plan.yaml` instance template and examples. The `hfk init` interview writes its starter rows to `out/hfk-plan-starter.csv` with the thirteen sheet headers, and the new one-time `hfk plan to-sheet` converts an existing `plan.yaml` to the same CSV. `hfk dev fixture` now writes the example `HFK Plan` sheet by default (`--no-plan-tab` leaves it out). The example plan is a Markdown table in `examples/instance/README.md`. Plan commands, `renewals` included, now need the workbook.
- M5-16 (ADR-0014): closure moves to the account entry. A `household.yaml` account takes optional `closed: YYYY-MM-DD` and `note` beside its `owner` or `shared` weights, and keeps those weights for every transaction, so a closed account with transactions in the reporting window scores without `ShareError`. Balances and net worth read the closure cutoff from the account entry. `hfk doctor`'s `closed-account-active` error now points at `household.yaml`, and `account-closed-and-open` is removed. `declarations.yaml` rejects `closed_accounts` with a fix that lists each entry to move. The `hfk init` interview asks who owned each closed account and writes it under `accounts` with `closed`; the example and template `declarations.yaml` files drop `closed_accounts: {}`.

### Added

- M10-01: [EXTENSIONS](docs/EXTENSIONS.md) gains a "Community extension candidates" section listing FSA and reimbursement tracking as out of core, with why, the shape of an extension, and its boundary with core.
- Proposed ADR-0018 asks the maintainer how tax enters the retirement income-vs-need gap: which streams are taxable, how fixed-dollar thresholds behave in today's dollars, and tax in the per-person view. Roadmap items `M8-05`, `M8-06` and `M10-02` are blocked until it is decided.
- M8-04 (R4): versioned federal tax tables (YAML tagged with `tax_year` and the publication `source`), a staleness gate on `max_data_age_years` (ADR-0005), and pluggable `none`, `flat` and `bracket` state-tax modules, with bracket and Social Security taxability arithmetic. No federal table ships: irs.gov could not be reached to verify the figures, so the extension fails loudly until the maintainer adds a cited table.
- M8-03 (R3): itemized retirement expenses in `retirement.yaml`, monthly in today's dollars and flat unless an item declares `real_growth` with a `reason` (ADR-0008); optional `who`, `start_year` and `end_year`. `seed_from_plan: {month: YYYY-MM}` starts from every `HFK Plan` line effective in that month, one expense per line.
- M8-02 (R2): typed retirement income streams in `retirement.yaml`: `social_security` with a benefit per claim age (62 to 70) and claim-age scenarios that pick one age per person, plus `pension`, `account_drawdown` and `other` with `monthly`, `start_year` and `end_year`. `cola` is required on every stream: `full`, `none`, or a nominal rate, converted to today's dollars with `inflation` as ADR-0008 sets out.
- M8-01 (R1): the built-in `retirement` extension, registered through this distribution's entry point, with its `retirement.yaml` schema: `people` (every household person, with `birth` year-month and `retire_year`) and `assumptions` (`until_age` required, `inflation` optional and capped at 0.20). `hfk doctor` checks it when the extension is enabled. Example: `examples/retirement.example.yaml`.
- Proposed ADR-0017 asks the maintainer who paid a shared account's charges in a settle-up statement, which decides the net transfer. Roadmap items `M7-04` to `M7-07` are blocked until it is decided.
- M7-03: off-ledger shared items in `shared-items/YYYY-MM.yaml` for settle-up, validated for amount, `paid_by`, `weights` (people from `household.yaml`, summing to exactly 1), and bucket. `hfk doctor` reports every bad file, including one not named after a month. The instance's `shared-items/README.md` now explains the format.
- M7-02: `hfk.ext.settle_up.shares.on_ledger_lines` lists each spend transaction in a month with what each person owes, read from the core share function, so settle-up declares no sharing rules of its own. For every fixture month, each person's total equals their scorecard total to the cent.
- M7-01: the built-in `settle_up` extension is registered through this distribution's `hfk.extensions` entry point, and `hfk doctor` errors when it is enabled for a household without exactly two people. Installed extensions' `commands()` now become `hfk` subcommands, run with an `hfk.ext.CommandContext` once core has checked that the extension is enabled and loaded its config; core command names are reserved. The example and template `household.yaml` enable `settle_up` again.
- M6-05: [EXTENSIONS](docs/EXTENSIONS.md) documents the implemented extension API: discovery, the contract with a checked minimal example, config files, doctor hooks, a boundaries table for public and private extension packages, testing without pip, and that built-in-first extensions ship inside `hfk.ext` but are still discovered through entry points.
- M6-04: `hfk doctor` runs extension doctor hooks after its core checks. `run_doctor` takes `hooks` (`hfk.engine.DoctorHook`, given a `DoctorContext`), so the engine never imports an extension; `hfk.ext.doctor.extension_doctor_hooks` builds them. A failing hook is an `extension-hook-failed` error naming the extension, and every other check still reports. The example and template `household.yaml` now enable no extensions, because an enabled extension that is not installed is an error.
- M6-03: extension config sections. `hfk.ext.load_enabled_extensions` reads and validates the own config file of each extension enabled in `household.yaml`, and never opens the file of one that is not enabled. An enabled extension that is not installed, lacks a required extension, or has a missing or invalid file is a `ConfigError` naming the file, key path, and fix.
- M6-02: the extension contract. `hfk.ext.validate_extension` checks `name`, `version`, `contract_version` (major 1), `config_section`, `schema`, `requires`, `commands()`, optional `report_sections()`, and `doctor`, and reports every broken member at once; `hfk.ext.load_extensions()` discovers and validates. `hfk.ext` adds `ExtensionCommand` and `ReportSection`. Proposed ADR-0016 asks the maintainer whether an extension's config may live inside `household.yaml` or `declarations.yaml`; until then it is a file of its own.
- M6-01: `hfk.ext.discover_extensions()` discovers extensions through the `hfk.extensions` entry-point group. An entry point may name the extension object, its class, or a factory function. A package that fails to load is reported by entry-point name without stopping the others.
- M5-17: `hfk init` folds in the findings of the `M3-07` real-data run. An expense group can be answered `non-spend`: it is written to `non_spend_groups` with no bucket and no starter plan line. The interview finds a Google Sheets download (`<owner's name> Tiller Spreadsheet - <created date>.xlsx`) beside `Tiller*.xls*` and writes a `tiller_glob` that finds it. When `data/` holds several workbooks, init names the one it read and lists the others, and `--workbook <path>` chooses another one in `data/`. `hfk.source` gains `find_workbooks` and `workbook_sort_key`.
- M5-13: [WORKING-WITH-AI](docs/WORKING-WITH-AI.md) for households: three ways to work (no AI, ask an AI for tweaks, develop the toolkit), the rule that an assistant may read the config and the code but never the workbook or statements, what to paste into a chat and what never to paste, copyable prompts, and how to review an AI-made change. `hfk init` now writes an instance `AGENTS.md` with that rule and a `CLAUDE.md` pointer to it.
- M5-12: one-line install. `tests/test_packaging.py` builds the wheel, installs it into a fresh venv outside the checkout, and runs `hfk --version`, `hfk doctor` on the example instance, and `hfk open --no-browser` with no source tree present; it also checks `uv tool install` of the wheel. The README gains an Install section naming `uv tool install household-finance-kit` (or `pipx install`) as the only terminal step, with `uv tool install .` from a checkout until the package is published (`M0-07`). `build` joins the dev extras.
- M5-11: `hfk open --home <dir>` starts `hfk serve` on a free loopback port and opens the browser on it (`--no-browser` only prints the address); it exits 0 when stopped with Ctrl+C or SIGTERM. `hfk init` writes two double-click launchers into the instance, `Open HFK.command` for macOS and Linux and `Open HFK.bat` for Windows, each running `hfk open` on the folder it sits in. The launchers are tested on Linux only; they have not been double-clicked on a real macOS or Windows desktop.
- M5-10: `hfk serve --home <dir>` is a local read-only web page for the reports. It binds `127.0.0.1` only and refuses any other `--host`. It serves the HTML scorecard, categories, balances, net worth, renewals and doctor pages with a month picker, a person picker and a Regenerate button that re-reads the workbook and saves the month's pages to `out/`. Every request method but GET gets 405, and a request with a foreign `Host` header gets 400. `hfk.render` gains `render_doctor_html`.
- M5-09: `hfk doctor` ends with a readiness section for new users. Four checks each say `ready` or `not ready` with a plain-language fix: the workbook has the `Transactions`, `Categories` and `Balance History` sheets; how many months of transactions fall on or after `history_start`, and how many of them are finished; how many expense categories still have no bucket; and whether the plan loads, from which source and with how many lines. Readiness never changes the exit code, which still comes only from errors.
- M5-08: the `HFK Plan` workbook sheet is now a plan source. `household.yaml` accepts `plan: {source: file | workbook}`, and `file` is the default. Under `workbook`, `scorecard`, `categories`, `renewals`, `rebaseline`, and `hfk doctor` read the plan from the sheet. The sheet is read by header name through the read-only loader and gives the same `Plan` lines, and the same cents, as `plan.yaml`. `hfk plan check` validates either source and names the sheet, row, column, and fix for each bad cell. A leftover `plan.yaml` gets a warning. `hfk dev fixture --plan-tab` writes the example plan into the synthetic workbook.
- M5-07: the `HFK Plan` workbook-tab contract (planned, M5-08), documented identically in the design brief's section 6, [TILLER-SCHEMA](docs/TILLER-SCHEMA.md), and [DECISIONS](docs/DECISIONS.md): an optional, user-added sheet read read-only by header name with the thirteen columns (`Description`, `Who`, `Bucket`, `Monthly`, `Categories` separated by `;`, `Paced`, `Effective From`, `Effective Until`, `Annual Bill`, `Annual Full`, `Account`, `Due Month`, `Due Day`), selected through `household.yaml`'s `plan: {source: file | workbook}` with `plan.yaml` as the version-controlled alternative, and recorded as ADR-0013, which supersedes the M10 `hfk plan edit` editor.
- M5-06: `hfk init <dir>` becomes an interview when `data/` already holds a Tiller workbook: init opens the newest one read-only, proposes `household.yaml` accounts from `Balance History` and `declarations.yaml` group-to-bucket mappings from the `Categories` sheet, asks only what the workbook cannot answer (the people and their slugs, which accounts are shared and the split, the bucket profile, the bucket for each expense group, `history_start`) on the terminal or non-interactively through `--answers <file>`, and writes the three YAML files with a comment per judgement call plus a starter `plan.yaml`, while plain `hfk init` stays byte-identical when `data/` holds no workbook.
- M5-05: `hfk.render.html` renders the scorecard, categories, balances, net worth, and renewals reports as static self-contained HTML pages — one inline stylesheet, no script, no external asset, the same row cells as the CSV report and the same totals as the text report with `Decimal` formatted only at the boundary through `money` — the five tabular commands gain `--format html` matching the six snapshots under `tests/snapshots/html/` byte for byte while text and CSV output stay unchanged, and `render_html` dispatches by report type and refuses unknown objects.
- M5-04: `hfk.render.csv` renders the scorecard, categories, balances, net worth, and renewals reports as one CSV table each — rows mirror the engine's frozen row dataclasses field for field with no presentation-only totals and `Decimal` values formatted only at the output boundary through `money` — the five tabular commands gain `--format csv` matching the six snapshots under `tests/snapshots/csv/` byte for byte while the default text output is unchanged, and `render_csv` dispatches by report type and refuses unknown objects through the same registry pattern as the text renderer.
- ADR-0014 and roadmap item `M5-16` (2026-10-17): a closed account keeps its ownership. Closure becomes an optional `closed: YYYY-MM-DD` (plus `note`) on the `household.yaml` account entry, so a card closed mid-year still scores its in-window transactions without `ShareError` and `hfk doctor` no longer reports it as declared twice; `declarations.yaml` will reject `closed_accounts` with a fix naming the move once `M5-16` lands.
- M5-03: `hfk.render.text` renders the scorecard, categories, balances, net worth, and renewals reports to the exact terminal text the CLI prints (byte-identical, checked against the six snapshots under `tests/snapshots/text/`), and `cli.py` now prints only what the renderer returns through the `render_text` registry that later CSV/HTML renderers and extension reports plug into.

- M5-02: `hfk init` wires the Tiller read-only guard into every private instance: it copies the guard byte-for-byte to `.claude/hooks/tiller_guard.py` (executable), writes `.claude/settings.json` with a `PreToolUse` hook that runs it through `$CLAUDE_PROJECT_DIR`, and gitignores `.claude/settings.local.json` while keeping `settings.json` and the hook tracked, all from packaged `hfk.templates` data so no toolkit path is touched.

- M5-01: `hfk init <dir> [--git]` creates a private instance skeleton — the three example YAML files, a plain-words README, a `.gitignore` blocking workbooks and reports, `shared-items/`, and empty gitignored `data/` and `out/` directories — without requiring `git` on `PATH`, refusing to overwrite any existing file, while `--git` additionally runs `git init` and installs a privacy pre-commit hook that blocks staged workbooks, CSV/TSV, PDF, and Numbers files and anything under `data/` or `out/`.
- M5-00: the three bucket presets ship as package data under `src/hfk/profiles/` and are carried in the wheel via `[tool.setuptools.package-data]`; `load_declarations` resolves `profile:` names against the packaged directory with `importlib.resources` when no `profiles_dir` is given, the unknown-profile error names every available profile, `available_profiles()` lists the packaged or explicit profile names, and a test keeps the packaged copies byte-identical to the documented top-level `profiles/` directory.
- M5 reshaped for a non-programmer household (2026-10-17): `hfk init` becomes a workbook-driven interview (`M5-06`), the plan can live in a read-only `HFK Plan` workbook tab (`M5-07`, `M5-08`), a doctor readiness section (`M5-09`), the local web front door and double-click launcher move from M10 into M5 (`M5-10`, `M5-11`), one-line install (`M5-12`), a working-with-AI guide (`M5-13`), a usability session (`M5-14`), and the README rewrite (`M5-15`). `M10-04` and `M10-06` are removed; `M3-07` now follows the interview so the parity check runs on an interview-built instance. The README milestone table marks M4 done.
- M4-06: `hfk plan check [--home DIR]` validates `plan.yaml` against `declarations.yaml` and the household's people without opening the workbook and writes nothing: a file-level failure reports exactly one problem, every `plan.lines` entry is checked independently with `load_plan`'s own rules so all bad lines are named at once, and lines that pass are checked for one person's category on two lines effective in the same month and for overrides, splits, or explicit `categories` mappings that disagree with a line's bucket; each row names the physical line, key path, and fix, and a clean plan prints `ok: <N> lines`.
- M4-05: `hfk rebaseline --who SLUG [--months N] [--through YYYY-MM]` proposes a monthly target per spend category from one person's share of actual spend over the last closed months, next to the current plan target from `plan.yaml`; categories nonzero in fewer than half the window months are `spiky` and get no proposal, categories whose largest month exceeds twice a positive median are `lumpy` and carry a max-vs-median flag, and everything else is `stable` with the mean proposed; the window is clamped to `history_start` with a note, and unclosed months, fewer than two months after the clamp, `--months` below 2, and an unknown or missing `--who` fail loudly. The command proposes only and never edits `plan.yaml`.
- M4-04: `hfk renewals [--month YYYY-MM] [--who SLUG]` schedules the plan's annual bills by their next due month and reports sinking-fund adequacy from `plan.yaml` and `household.yaml` alone, never opening the workbook; each fund cycles yearly at its due month (a contribution every month including the due month, the bill paid from the full fund, the cycle restarting after), `history_start` and the lines' effective months limit accrual, undated lines, undeclared accounts, owner shares that disagree with account weights, and lines ending before their next due month are flagged rather than fixed, and months before `plan.effective_from` or `history_start` fail loudly.
- M4-03: `hfk networth --as-of YYYY-MM-DD [--stale-after-days N]` sums the per-account balances `hfk balances` selects into assets and liabilities by Tiller `Class`; gaps and unclassified accounts are excluded with a warning rather than counted as zero, closed accounts stop counting at their declared closure date, liabilities are summed as recorded and flagged when positive, synced accounts are flagged stale past the threshold (default 14 days), accounts whose Balance History is hand-typed are declared per account with `unsynced: true` in `household.yaml` and never flagged stale, and an `--as-of` before `history_start` or with no observations fails loudly.
- M4-02: `hfk balances --as-of YYYY-MM-DD` reports every account's latest `Balance History` observation on or before the date, where a blank or missing observation is a gap with its last known value and date rather than zero or a silently promoted older number; observations before `history_start` are ignored and noted, closed accounts stop at their declared closure date, undeclared accounts are flagged, and an `--as-of` before `history_start` fails loudly.
- M4-01: `hfk categories --month YYYY-MM` reports one person's plan lines with `categories` against actual ledger spend; `paced` lines are prorated to the elapsed window mid-month and to `history_start` clamps while fixed lines keep their full monthly target, mid-month is an explicitly paced view rather than a projection, and duplicate categories, unstarted months, and a missing `--who` fail loudly.
